// Installation

It installs like an app.
But there's no store and nothing to download.

BaseCero is a website your browser keeps as an application: its own icon, full screen and offline support. It takes ten seconds and asks for nothing in return.

Android

Chrome · Edge · Brave

  1. Open basecero.alvarotc.com/app/ in Chrome.
  2. Accept the “Install app” prompt if it shows up.
  3. If it doesn't: menu ⋮ → “Add to Home screen”.

iPhone and iPad

Safari only

  1. Open the app in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button in the bottom bar.
  3. Scroll down and choose “Add to Home Screen”.

Desktop

Chrome · Edge

  1. Open the app in Chrome or Edge.
  2. Click the install icon on the right of the address bar.
  3. Confirm “Install BaseCero”.

On iPhone it has to be Safari. iOS doesn't allow installing web apps from Chrome or Firefox. That's an Apple limitation, not a BaseCero one.

Once installed, open it from its icon and it works offline. To uninstall it, just delete the icon — and your data goes with it.

// How it works

Your month starts the day you get paid.

Not on the 1st. BaseCero orders money into payday-to-payday periods, and everything else falls into place from there: the budget, what's actually available and the expenses you split.

The four promises

01 / 04

Local, always

No server and no sign-up. Your data lives in a database inside your browser. It works in airplane mode.

02 / 04

Your data is a spreadsheet

Export and import as .xlsx whenever you want. Nothing gets locked inside the app.

03 / 04

Payday-to-payday periods

The budget and the “actually available” figure follow your pay cycle, not the calendar.

04 / 04

Shared expenses and budgets

Settle up the expenses you split with someone else and keep every category under its limit, period after period.

// Questions

What people
usually ask.

Missing one? Ask in the repository: they get answered there, in the open.

Ask a question
Where's my data?

On your device, in a local database in the browser (OPFS). BaseCero has no server: nothing leaves that database unless you export it.

How do I back up?

Export a plain .xlsx (to open in Google Sheets or LibreOffice) or a password-encrypted .bce copy. You decide when and where to keep them.

Which banks can I import from?

N26 has a direct import profile with deduplication. For any other bank, a generic assistant maps the columns of its CSV export.

What does it cost, and what's the license?

It's free and open source, under the MIT license. You're free to read, modify and reuse the code.

Does it have analytics or cookies?

None, by design. There's no server to receive anything you do in the app, so there's no cookie banner either — there's nothing to consent to.

What if I lose my device?

The data lives only there. If you hadn't exported a backup first, it's gone with the device — that's why it's worth exporting copies regularly.
